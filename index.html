Study Skills Framework and Guide

Enhancing Learning Outcomes for NSW Public High School Students (Years 7-12)

10-Week Instructional Program

Introduction

This workbook is designed to help you develop essential study skills that will support your learning throughout high school and beyond. Each week focuses on a different aspect of effective learning, with practical activities and evidence-based strategies. By completing this program, you'll gain tools to become a more confident, efficient, and successful learner.

Week 1: Goal Setting and Planning

Key Concepts:

  • SMART goals (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound)
  • Backward planning from assessment dates
  • Prioritization techniques (Eisenhower Matrix)

Activities:

SMART Goal Workshop

Transform vague goals ("do better in math") into SMART goals ("improve my algebra test scores by 10% by the end of term through completing 3 extra practice problems daily").

Study Schedule Creation

Using digital tools (Google Calendar, Notion) or paper planners to block out study times, accounting for energy levels throughout the day.

Exemplar:

"My SMART goal: Achieve a Band 5 in my next Science assessment by studying 45 minutes daily, completing all set homework, and attending two after-school tutorials before the exam date of May 15th."

Week 2: Effective Note-Taking

Key Concepts:

  • Cornell Note-Taking Method (Cue Column, Notes, Summary)
  • Visual note-taking strategies (mind maps, flow charts)
  • Active listening techniques for lecture capture

Activities:

Cornell Note Practice

Watch a short educational video and take notes using the Cornell method, then exchange with a peer to compare approaches.

Concept Mapping

Create mind maps for current History topics showing relationships between events, causes, and consequences using colours and images.

Exemplar:

"Effective notes from a Biology lesson on photosynthesis include: diagrams of chloroplast structure, key terms defined in the margin, and a summary connecting the process to cellular respiration."

Week 3: Active Learning Techniques

Key Concepts:

  • Bloom's Taxonomy question stems
  • Retrieval practice benefits
  • Peer teaching effectiveness (Protege Effect)

Activities:

Question Generation

Create quiz questions at different cognitive levels (recall, application, analysis) for a recent Math topic and exchange with classmates.

Teach-Back Sessions

In pairs, explain a concept from English literature to your partner as if they were a younger student, then receive feedback on clarity.

Exemplar:

"Sample higher-order question for Chemistry: 'How would you design an experiment to test the effect of temperature on reaction rates, controlling for other variables?'"

Week 4: Memory and Retention Strategies

Key Concepts:

  • Spaced repetition schedules (Anki flashcards)
  • Mnemonic devices (acronyms, method of loci)
  • Dual coding theory (combining verbal and visual)

Activities:

Spaced Repetition Demo

Use digital flashcard apps to demonstrate optimal review intervals for vocabulary retention in Languages.

Mnemonic Creation

Develop memory aids for challenging sequences (e.g., mathematical order of operations, historical timelines).

Exemplar:

"For remembering the planets: My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune)."

Week 5: Critical Thinking and Problem Solving (Part 1)

Key Concepts:

  • Socratic questioning techniques
  • Problem decomposition strategies
  • Identifying assumptions and biases

Activities:

Socratic Seminar

Discuss a current issue in Geography using probing questions: "What evidence supports this view?" "What alternative explanations exist?"

Case Study Analysis

Work in groups to analyze an environmental challenge, considering stakeholders, constraints, and potential solutions.

Exemplar:

"When examining media reports, critical thinkers ask: Who benefits from this narrative? What information might be missing? How does this compare to other sources?"

Week 6: Critical Thinking and Problem Solving (Part 2)

Key Concepts:

  • Algorithmic vs. heuristic approaches
  • Lateral thinking techniques
  • Evaluating solution effectiveness

Activities:

Complex Problem Scenarios

Tackle multi-step Physics problems using structured approaches (identify knowns/unknowns, select equations, check units).

Solution Evaluation

Compare different approaches to a Design challenge, assessing pros/cons of each method.

Exemplar:

"In solving algebraic equations, systematic approaches (balance method) often prove more reliable than trial-and-error, especially with complex problems."

Week 7: Exam Preparation Techniques (Part 1)

Key Concepts:

  • Testing effect and desirable difficulties
  • Marking rubric analysis
  • Stress inoculation through practice

Activities:

Past Paper Analysis

Deconstruct sample answers to identify what constitutes a Band 6 response in English essays.

Timed Practice

Complete a shortened exam under timed conditions to build pacing awareness.

Exemplar:

"Top-performing students typically: review past papers early, identify recurring question types, and practice writing responses within time limits."

Week 8: Exam Preparation Techniques (Part 2)

Key Concepts:

  • Time allocation strategies
  • Error analysis and pattern recognition
  • Performance review protocols

Activities:

Exam Simulation

Complete a full-length practice exam at home under exam conditions, then bring marked papers for review.

Time Management Drill

Practice allocating time across different question types based on mark value and difficulty.

Exemplar:

"For a 2-hour exam with 3 sections: spend 5 minutes planning, allocate 35 minutes per major essay, and reserve 20 minutes for review."

Week 9: Feedback and Reflection

Key Concepts:

  • Growth mindset principles
  • Feedback interpretation frameworks
  • Metacognitive reflection techniques

Activities:

Feedback Decoding

Translate teacher comments into specific improvement actions (e.g., "develop arguments further" = add counterarguments).

Learning Journal

Maintain reflective entries tracking study strategy effectiveness and personal growth.

Exemplar:

"Reflective entry: My spaced repetition helped with Chemistry formulas, but I need to apply concepts more in practice questions as suggested in my feedback."

Week 10: Supporting Wellbeing and Resilience

Key Concepts:

  • Stress response physiology
  • Productive rest principles
  • Attention restoration theory

Activities:

Mindfulness Practice

Guided breathing exercises and body scans to develop focus and stress management skills.

Study-Life Balance Plan

Create personalised schedules incorporating adequate sleep, physical activity, and social connection.

Exemplar:

"Effective students often follow the 50/10 rule: 50 minutes of focused study followed by 10 minutes of complete break (walking, stretching, not screens)."
